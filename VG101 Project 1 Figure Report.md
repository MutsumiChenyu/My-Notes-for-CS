## Figure 1. Crime Description Word Cloud

  



  

This word cloud summarizes frequent secondary crime descriptions in 2025. Larger phrases represent descriptions that appear more often in the dataset. Terms such as `SIMPLE`, `DOMESTIC BATTERY SIMPLE`, `RETAIL THEFT`, `TO VEHICLE`, `$500 AND UNDER`, and `OVER $500` are especially prominent, indicating that simple battery, theft, vehicle-related offenses, and property-value-based theft descriptions are common in the 2025 records.

  

## Figure 2. Yearly Reported Crimes

  



  

This bar chart shows the annual number of reported crimes in Chicago from 2014 to 2025. The number of reported cases stayed relatively high before 2019, dropped noticeably in 2020 and 2021, and then recovered in 2022-2024. The 2025 value is lower than 2023 and 2024, suggesting a recent decrease in reported crimes.

  

## Figure 3. Average Accumulated Monthly Crimes

  



  

This line plot shows the average accumulated number of crimes by month from 2014 to 2025. The curve increases steadily from January to December, which means crime reports accumulate at a fairly regular pace throughout the year. By December, the average accumulated total is about 255,000 cases.

  

## Figure 4. Primary Crime Type Distribution

  



  

This pie chart presents the top primary crime types from 2014 to 2025. `THEFT` is the largest category, followed by `BATTERY`, while `CRIMINAL DAMAGE`, `ASSAULT`, `DECEPTIVE PRACTICE`, and `OTHER OFFENSE` also account for large portions. The sizable `others` category shows that many less frequent types still contribute substantially when combined.

  

## Figure 5. Crime Time Pattern

  



  

This heatmap shows the distribution of 2025 crimes by day of week and hour of day. Brighter colors indicate more reported crimes. The highest activity appears around midnight and during afternoon-to-evening hours, while early morning hours around 4-6 are generally lower. The pattern suggests that crime reports are concentrated in specific time periods rather than evenly distributed.

  

## Figure 6. Crime Location Density with Community Area Boundaries

  



  

This map shows the geographic density of 2025 crime records together with Chicago community area boundaries. Darker density regions indicate stronger concentrations of reported crimes. The boundary labels help connect high-density regions with specific community areas, making the spatial pattern easier to interpret than raw coordinates alone.

  

## Figure 7. Crime Distribution by Police District

  



  

This geobubble map shows the number of 2025 crimes by police district. Larger bubbles represent districts with more reported crimes, and the map reveals clear differences across districts. Some districts have around fifteen thousand reported cases, while others have far fewer, showing that crime distribution is uneven across Chicago police districts.
